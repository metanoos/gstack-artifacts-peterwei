## Problem Statement

Zine needs social and collaborative interfaces that preserve the product's
central claim: writing is not only a final document. It is a signed history of
choices, sources, revisions, discarded alternatives, attention, and voices.

The initial collaboration direction drifted toward a Google Docs or Twitch
architecture: synchronized editors, live cursors, stages, collaborator rosters,
and remotely shared mutable state. That added authority, conflict-resolution,
identity, privacy, and network concepts faster than it added value.

The revised product separates five acts:

1. **Record** fine-grained production and consumption locally.
2. **Step** a deliberate, signed local checkpoint.
3. **Send or Publish** that unchanged checkpoint to an audience.
4. **Replay** its committed process asynchronously.
5. **Reply** from an independently owned trace.

The first pass of Replay is ordinary reading. The reader begins with the
materialized text, not a player, timeline, or generated highlight reel. Because
the text remains addressable into its committed trace, the reader can disclose
provenance, prior wording, voices, and causal history only where curiosity calls
for it. Stepping, animation, ranking, and model guidance are deeper renderers of
that same reading experience.

Public interaction defaults to Reply and Replay. An owner may separately grant
a pubkey permission to construct inside a file, folder, or recursive subtree.
Permissioned contributors still work asynchronously. There is no requirement
for a live shared editor, presence, cursor streaming, or keystroke networking.

The same process history must also solve the prompt-context problem. Replay
starts by reading the trace-backed text and never depends on a ranking. When a
reader opens the history or a model is asked for guidance, the moments worth
identifying and the moments worth supplying as prompt context are the same
addressable episodes over the same operation and observation streams. Optional
Replay guidance and prompt injection use one visible salience structure rather
than contradictory hidden rankings.

## What Makes This Cool

The finished essay is only one projection. Zine preserves the movement that
produced it:

- a paragraph revised nine times across three sessions;
- a sentence restored after two attempts to replace it;
- a collaborator rewriting one phrase inside another voice's larger passage;
- a forty-minute pause before a decisive cut;
- a citation opened, read, and carried into a later reply;
- a model suggestion that returns toward wording the author previously left;
- two mobile contributors working offline from one Step and interleaving later.

That process becomes:

- an authorship ledger without pretending to prove humanness;
- an asynchronous writing performance people can replay;
- a source of unusually good prompt context;
- a deletion-aware memory that retrieval over final text cannot recreate;
- a correspondence surface where people and models use the same provenance
  grammar without receiving misleading identities.

The "Twitch for writing" analogy describes the fidelity of the recorded
performance, not the transport. The network publishes chapters. The rich
performance is fetched and replayed after a Step is sent.

## Constraints

1. `Step` is local. It signs and advances local state but does not publish.
2. `Send` or `Publish` distributes an existing Step without changing its id,
   content, roots, voices, or signer.
3. Fine-grained operations and attention events never require one Nostr event
   or signature per keystroke.
4. Public users receive Reply and Replay. Direct construction requires an
   explicit pubkey-scoped capability.
5. A pubkey always denotes an entity that actually signs. A voice id does not.
6. Files and folders share provenance and commitment grammar but use different
   reducers.
7. Material operations, observations, publication, access, and retention are
   separate concerns.
8. The current target is mandatory prompt context. Other context must be
   inspectable, bounded, and reproducible.
9. A folder page and its Navigator row expose the same direct members one for
   one. Projections never silently change membership.
10. The design must work offline and tolerate mobile devices that sleep,
    reconnect, and submit work later.
11. The first experiment must not depend on a new network or wire-format
    migration.

## Premises

1. Interleaved voices do not imply a synchronous shared editor.
2. Single-owner governance does not imply a single producing voice.
3. An owner can pre-authorize future work without transferring ownership.
4. Signed checkpoints and signed contributor authority are different claims.
5. A model voice can be attributed honestly without inventing a model pubkey.
6. Deletion obtains meaning from its replacement and surrounding trajectory,
   not from a binary deleted/not-deleted label.
7. High-fidelity production and consumption telemetry is part of the product,
   not incidental exhaust to minimize away.
8. Recording locally and disclosing publicly are different acts.
9. Deterministic convergence preserves contributions but does not resolve
   editorial intent. Replay must make collisions legible.
10. A raw edit log is the wrong default prompt representation. The product
    advantage is a faithful reduction into verifiable episodes.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent architecture critique supplied during the session made four
useful corrections:

1. A signed event must commit to an operation prefix rather than contain every
   prior operation. Cumulative snapshots of the log would become quadratic.
2. A zine checkpoint needs its own trace commitment. A node-wide feed length
   does not prove that one zine's served history is complete.
3. High-rate operations belong in an append-only Merkle log. Low-rate
   publications, citations, and discovery objects belong in Nostr.
4. Real editing traces already exist, so the reducer and episode compiler can
   be tested before the editor or network is changed.

This design accepts that boundary and adds two qualifications:

- operation identities do not make an arbitrary range self-sufficient; an
  episode still needs a causal frontier plus closure or a verified keyframe;
- optional Replay guidance and prompt injection share one episode compiler and
  visible salience structure, while direct Replay remains complete and
  model-independent.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Live collaborative Stage

Use Yjs-style synchronized documents, awareness, cursors, collaborator rosters,
and capability-controlled direct editing. Stream fine-grained changes to peers.

**Effort:** XL

**Risk:** High

**Pros**

- Familiar multiplayer editing behavior.
- Immediate awareness of collaborators.
- Existing client code already contains CodeMirror and Yjs collaboration work.

**Cons**

- Turns the product into a shared-state service before proving Replay or prompt
  context.
- Requires always-on coordination, conflict UX, presence privacy, mobile
  background networking, and authority semantics.
- Makes the Stage a competing product concept instead of a projection of a
  zine's signed history.

**Decision:** Rejected as the product model.

### Approach B: Strict single-owner correspondence

Every external contribution is an independently owned Reply. The target owner
may quote or adopt it locally, but no other key can write anywhere inside an
owned tree.

**Effort:** Small

**Risk:** Low

**Pros**

- Very small authority surface.
- Reply, Replay, citation, and independent traces remain sufficient for public
  correspondence.
- No concurrent reducer is required for normal editing.

**Cons**

- Cannot express a shared research or publishing folder whose owner lets
  trusted people build freely.
- Treats interleaved human voices as exceptional while accepting interleaved
  human and model voices locally.
- Gives mobile collaborators no path beyond suggestions and manual adoption.

**Decision:** Rejected as too restrictive.

### Approach C: Asynchronous capability-scoped construction

Keep Reply and Replay as the public grammar. Let an owner issue signed,
pubkey-scoped capabilities for asynchronous construction inside an exact file,
folder, or subtree. Authorized contributors produce causal operation groups
offline and send them later. An owner or delegated signer checkpoints the
accepted frontier.

**Effort:** Large

**Risk:** Medium

**Pros**

- Supports mobile, offline, human, model, and multi-device work without a live
  shared editor.
- Preserves single-owner governance while allowing several producing voices and
  real delegated authority.
- Gives identity-aware file and folder reducers a concrete concurrency problem
  to solve.
- Keeps public social behavior simple for everyone without a capability.

**Cons**

- Requires a precise capability and revocation model.
- Same-file offline concurrency needs deterministic reduction plus an honest
  conflict interface.
- The physical mapping from multiple device feeds into one accepted zine
  frontier needs a prototype before becoming wire law.

**Decision:** Chosen.

## Recommended Approach

### 1. Product grammar

```text
Local authoring:
  Edit or observe -> Step

Distribution:
  Step -> Send or Publish

Public correspondence:
  Receive -> Replay -> Reply -> Step -> Send

Permissioned construction:
  Grant -> work offline -> submit operation groups
        -> reduce accepted frontier -> Step -> Send
```

Transport may deliver an immutable object quickly, but no remote object depends
on a synchronized session.

### 2. State model

A zine is a replayable state machine:

```ts
type ZineStateMachine = {
  reducer: "file-text" | "folder-membership";
  operationGroups: OperationGroup[];
  keyframes: Keyframe[];
  steps: StepCheckpoint[];
};
```

The shared layer provides:

- causal operation and group identities;
- target zine and reducer kind;
- actor, voice, executor, and signer provenance;
- command, query, and model-call receipts;
- keyframes and prefix commitments;
- Replay addressing;
- deterministic validation and resource limits.

The reducers remain different.

### 3. File operations

File text uses identity- and frontier-aware insert and delete operations grouped
at editor-transaction or semantic-action boundaries. One user gesture may
contain several voice-homogeneous batches:

```ts
type OperationGroupView = {
  groupId: string;
  target: ZineRef;
  baseStep: StepRef;
  parentTips: OpId[];
  batches: VoiceBatch[];
  cause?: ReceiptRef;
};

type VoiceBatch = {
  voiceId: VoiceId;
  replicaId: ReplicaId;
  operations: FileOperation[];
  submittedBy?: Pubkey;
  capability?: CapabilityRef;
  evidence?: EvidenceRef[];
};
```

`replicaId` exists for uniqueness and convergence. It is not a persona, author,
or pubkey. Operations inherit batch provenance rather than repeating it per
character.

An immutable batch is never edited in place. If voice B replaces text inserted
by voice A, the log retains A's insertion, B's deletion, and B's replacement.
The current visible attribution is a derived run such as A/B/A. A deletion-only
contribution remains visible in Replay even though it owns no surviving text.

### 4. Folder operations

A folder is an ordered set of stable membership entries:

```ts
type FolderOperation =
  | {
      type: "member.add";
      entryId: string;
      child: ZineRef;
      after?: string;
      moveId?: string;
    }
  | { type: "member.remove"; entryId: string; moveId?: string }
  | { type: "member.advance"; entryId: string; childHead: StepRef }
  | { type: "order.move"; entryId: string; after?: string }
  | {
      type: "order.resolve";
      entryIds: string[];
      resolves: OpId[];
    };
```

Folder operations use the same operation groups, voices, capabilities,
commitments, and receipts as file operations. They do not use a text reducer or
file editor transactions.

Accepted query population is a group of ordinary `member.add` operations linked
to the exact query receipt. Cross-folder movement is not atomic across two
independently stepped folder traces. It uses two uniquely identified,
target-specific groups linked by one `moveId`: a source removal and a
destination addition. Either half may arrive first. The UI shows a pending move
until both accepted Steps are available, and Replay retains a partial or
cancelled move rather than pretending atomicity.

The ordered folder snapshot is authoritative. The custom Stack order is
governance-authored by the owner or a signer holding scoped `order` authority;
the UI identifies which signer accepted it. Alphabetical, recency, citation
count, and activity rate are derived viewer sorts until an authorized signer
explicitly accepts a displayed permutation as the new custom order.

### 5. Voices, signers, and evidence

The system distinguishes four roles:

```ts
type ContributionProvenance = {
  producedBy: VoiceId;
  executedBy: ReplicaId | ToolId;
  submittedBy?: Pubkey;
  acceptedBy?: Pubkey;
  signedBy?: Pubkey;
  witnessedBy?: Pubkey[];
  derivedFrom?: SourceRef[];
};
```

- `voiceId` identifies a human, model, tool, or composite voice in provenance.
- A `pubkey` appears only when that key actually signs a claim.
- A human may bind a voice to their key.
- A model voice normally has provider, model, configuration, request, response,
  and witness evidence rather than a synthetic signer.
- A Step signer attests to inclusion. The signature does not retroactively claim
  that the signer produced every contained span.

A voice id should be stable and content-addressed from its genesis descriptor.
The descriptor may carry a controller pubkey and signed bindings with roles such
as `speaker`, `controller`, `provider`, or `witness`. Permissions are granted to
pubkeys, never to voice ids.

### 6. Capabilities and delegated construction

A capability is a signed grant:

```ts
type CapabilityView = {
  capabilityId: string;
  issuer: Pubkey;
  subject: Pubkey;
  scope: ZineRef;
  recursive: boolean;
  rights: Array<
    "create" | "write" | "order" | "step" | "send" | "publish"
  >;
  creationPolicy?: "inherit-governance" | "independent-child";
  issuedAtPolicy: { policySeq: number; policyHash: string };
  notAfterPolicySeq?: number;
};
```

Rights stay separate:

- `create` permits child creation inside a folder;
- `write` permits operation submission to existing targets;
- `order` permits membership or custom-order changes;
- `step` permits checkpoint signing in scope;
- `send` permits delivery to named recipients without public discovery;
- `publish` permits public relay and discovery distribution.

The grant supplies advance authorization. An owner does not need to approve
every batch from a contributor who has the necessary right. Revocation affects
future incorporation and never rewrites accepted history.

The root owner signs the canonical grant body. `capabilityId` is the signed
object id. V1 does not permit a capability holder to issue another capability;
recursive scope applies the owner's one grant to descendants without creating a
delegation chain. Later narrowing delegation requires a separate reviewed
schema.

The owner maintains a monotonically sequenced capability-policy log. A grant
becomes active only when an owner-signed policy record includes its exact id.
A revocation names one capability id and a policy sequence. Automatic inclusion
tests the selected policy head at the incorporating Step, not an untrusted submission timestamp.
Work received after a revocation is pending even when it was based on an older
Step where the capability was valid. The owner may still adopt it manually,
which creates a new authorized operation group. This intentionally chooses
safety over trying to prove when an offline device performed work.

```ts
type PolicyRecordBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "policy-record";
  owner: Pubkey;
  policySeq: number;
  priorPolicyHash: string;
  action:
    | { type: "activate-capability"; capabilityId: string }
    | { type: "revoke-capability"; capabilityId: string };
  reason?: string;
};
```

The owner signs every `PolicyRecordBody` in a `SignedEnvelope`. `policySeq` must be
exactly one greater than the preceding accepted owner policy record and
`priorPolicyHash` must match it. A forked or skipped policy sequence is invalid
until the owner selects one policy head. Wall-clock expiry may be displayed but
cannot authorize offline incorporation; `notAfterPolicySeq` is normative.

A Step is historically verifiable against the exact `{policySeq, policyHash}` it
names. Before selecting a TraceHead, sending, or publishing, the client also
checks the signer and capability against the owner's current selected
`PolicyHead`. A Step signed under a capability revoked by that current head
remains valid historical evidence but is ineligible for automatic head
selection or publication. The owner may incorporate its operations in a new
owner-authorized Step.

The root owner remains governance authority. In v1, only the root owner may
select the canonical head. If the owner retains exclusive
`step`, contributor work remains a signed pending frontier until an owner Step
includes it. If the owner delegates `step`, the delegate's checkpoint includes
the capability chain and does not imply ownership transfer. Concurrent
delegate-signed checkpoints form branches. The current canonical head is chosen
by the root owner; no timestamp or lexicographic-signature rule silently makes
an editorial choice.

Created child traces need an explicit policy:

- inherited governance, where the folder authority controls the child and the
  creator receives scoped rights; or
- independent child ownership, where the folder stores a reference to the
  creator's trace.

Any grant carrying `create` must name `creationPolicy`. It must not be inferred
from folder membership.

### 7. Mobile collaboration

The mobile unit is a signed offline contribution, not a persistent connection:

1. A device receives a capability, Step, and nearby keyframe.
2. It records operations and observations locally.
3. Its user or delegated device key signs a submitted frontier.
4. Send places the submission at a relay, mirror, or hosted peer.
5. The recipient later verifies the capability and causal base.
6. The reducer incorporates authorized operation groups.
7. An owner or delegated signer creates the next Step.
8. Other devices fetch and Replay the result.

Phones use revocable device keys delegated from a durable identity. The user's
voice id remains stable across devices while `executedBy` and `signedBy` reveal
which device acted.

A phone must not be an always-on host. Store-and-forward infrastructure keeps a
submission available while devices sleep. A signed submission envelope binds
the signer, target, base Step, operation-group Merkle root, capability id,
schema version, and a unique nonce. Its content-addressed id provides replay
protection and duplicate delivery is idempotent.

An automatically admissible submission must have a capability active in the
policy state of the Step that incorporates it. An owner may manually adopt late
work after revocation, creating a new explicit local act.

### 8. Local record, Step, and Publish

The local record contains two logical stream families:

1. **Material streams:** file or folder operations.
2. **Performance streams:** timing, selection, viewport, focus, dwell, tab and
   mounted-scope changes, opened citations, navigation, and human or model
   queries.

Performance capture preserves characteristic production and consumption
patterns. It is not limited to sparse selections around model calls. Events use
monotonic offsets plus a session wall-clock anchor so timing remains replayable
without placing a full wall-clock timestamp on every event.

`Step`:

- chooses exact stream prefixes;
- materializes the file or folder snapshot;
- records any keyframe;
- signs the snapshot hash and stream commitments;
- advances the local head;
- announces the transition to local Press processes only.

`Send` or `Publish`:

- transmits the existing signed Step event without mutating it;
- uploads or pins its promised ranges before advertising availability;
- distributes any audience-specific access grants;
- never changes the Step id or payload.

`Send` is addressed delivery. It names recipients, may carry wrapped content
keys, and does not add the Step to public discovery or update a public Profile.

`Publish` is stranger-readable distribution. It requires at least one selected
host to confirm the promised replay bundle is obtainable, advertises the Step
through publication relays, and may update public discovery or a separately
signed Profile pointer.

Both operations share an idempotent preparation prefix and then diverge:

```text
common:
  local -> ranges-pinned

Send:
  ranges-pinned -> addressed-delivered -> recipient-acknowledged

Publish:
  ranges-pinned -> relay-event-accepted
                -> discovery-updated
                -> stranger-fetch-verified
```

`ranges-pinned` means at least one selected host acknowledged every promised
bundle object. `addressed-delivered` means the recipient transport accepted the
immutable Step, host locators, and any key grants. `recipient-acknowledged` is an
optional signed delivery receipt and does not imply the recipient read it.
`relay-event-accepted` is a relay acknowledgment. `discovery-updated` means the
public index or optional Profile pointer references the Step.
`stranger-fetch-verified` means a read performed without the publisher's local
cache retrieved and verified the advertised public bundle.

`obtainable` and `decryptable` are independent status flags. A retry resumes
from the last confirmed state. The client does not advertise Replay as ready
before the minimum promised range host acknowledges the bundle. If availability
later degrades, the immutable Step remains valid while discovery displays the
missing host state and retries replication.

Several private Steps may accumulate before publication. Publishing a later Step
must make every promised causal range and Replay boundary obtainable, whether or
not every intermediate checkpoint is separately announced to every relay.

### 9. Hypercore and Nostr boundary

High-rate records live in append-only Merkle streams. Low-rate signed social and
publication objects live in Nostr.

A Step commits to prefixes rather than copying cumulative history:

```ts
type StreamCommitment = {
  streamId: string;
  coreKey: string;
  length: number;
  root: string;
};
```

The Step carries:

- zine and prior Step identity;
- materialized snapshot hash;
- accepted causal frontier;
- exact material and included performance stream commitments;
- optional keyframe reference;
- voice descriptor and evidence references needed by the Step;
- the checkpoint signer's Nostr signature.

Hypercore feed keys are storage identities. They are not author or voice
identities. The Nostr signer makes the authorship or delegated-authority claim.

The physical feed topology remains an experiment:

- one authority feed that ingests signed contributor submissions is simplest but
  makes the authority a sequencer;
- one feed per device or contributor permits offline construction but makes a
  Step commit an accepted set of feed frontiers;
- a higher-level multi-feed reducer may provide the same logical frontier.

The logical operation format must not depend on which topology wins.

Public, subscriber-gated, and private material may require different encrypted
streams or access epochs. Publication may wrap content keys for an audience.
No design may claim to revoke plaintext that a recipient already decrypted.

### 10. Episodes and revision trajectories

The deterministic episode compiler consumes both stream families:

```text
verified operations + performance observations
                    |
          deterministic reconstruction
                    |
          revision trajectories and features
                    |
         one addressable salience structure
             /                     \
          Replay              prompt context
```

Episode boundaries are derived behavioral and editorial boundaries. They do not
create Steps. Keyframe cadence is also independent and selected by measured seek
cost.

Deletion-awareness in v1 models trajectories:

```ts
type RevisionEdge = {
  from: SpanRef;
  to?: SpanRef;
  relation: "replace" | "move" | "restore" | "remove";
  delta: {
    elapsedTime?: number; // only when source timing passes the evidence audit
    causalEvents: number;
    surfaceDistance: number;
    positionalDistance: number;
    structuralDistance: number;
  };
  fromVoice: VoiceId;
  toVoice?: VoiceId;
};
```

The compiler links removed text to overlapping or causally nearby replacement,
movement, restoration, or terminal removal. It retains a vector rather than
prematurely collapsing every dimension into one score.

Useful derived measures include:

- total path length across revisions;
- net distance from first to final version;
- path-to-net ratio for oscillation;
- distance over time for edit velocity;
- changes in magnitude or direction for acceleration, reversal, and convergence;
- voice transitions across an edge.

Deterministic range overlap, causal distance, structure, position, and token
similarity establish the base linkage. Validated timing may add elapsed-time
features, but its absence never becomes a zero-duration edit. A model may
summarize or rerank linked evidence but must not invent the linkage or replace
exact range references.

Request scope may constrain eligible episodes. Prompt injection and optional
Replay guidance use the exact same versioned Episode IR and ranking function.
Prompt selection is a declared bounded projection over that order. Its frozen
manifest records scope, budget, selected ids, exclusions, and tie-breaks, so a
Replay guide can reproduce the exact prompt projection and direct the reader to
the complete underlying trace. A hidden prompt-only ranking must not contradict
the visible guidance.

### 11. Prompt-injection manager

The manager compiles `append`, `reply`, and `rewrite` requests against the
authorized mounted scope.

The current target is always mandatory. Other candidates come from:

- current signed heads of mounted zines;
- verified episode and trajectory references;
- exact citations and reply targets;
- explicitly permitted relay queries;
- human pins and exclusions.

The model receives reduced episode cards, not tens of thousands of raw events.
It may request a bounded drill-down into exact ranges. Every query and expansion
is recorded.

The frozen context manifest includes:

- exact target and mounted-scope heads;
- selected and excluded episode ids;
- stream commitments and range references;
- rendered bytes and budget;
- selector and renderer versions;
- model configuration;
- relay or trace-expansion queries and responses;
- request and resulting operation-group ids.

The Context Inspector lets the writer preview, pin, exclude, and open any
candidate at its exact Replay location; accelerated playback is an optional
renderer. The same address appears in the final model receipt.

Inline model commands remain narrow:

- `((append instruction))`
- `((reply instruction))`
- `((rewrite instruction))`

Typing the final `))` triggers the local action. A command is consumed only when
written with `!`, such as `((append! instruction))`, and only after accepted
success. Queries remain browse or folder actions rather than inline commands.

### 11A. Gate 1a execution contract

Gate 1a is the only approved implementation scope in this document.

**Inventory item zero is timestamp validity.** The public
[`editing-traces`](https://github.com/josephg/editing-traces) collection uses a
`time` field, but its meaning varies by trace:

- `seph-blog1` has 137,154 transactions, 26,744 distinct, monotonic,
  non-epoch timestamps from 2021-05-12 through 2021-08-10. It is the Gate 1a
  trace and may support pause and burst features.
- `automerge-paper` is explicitly documented as lacking timestamps. Its
  populated sub-second values span only 294 ms and are not composition time; it
  is structure-only evidence.
- `friendsforever` uses the Unix epoch for every transaction and is
  structure-only evidence.
- `clownschool` records one-second timing, but its flattened concurrent order is
  not globally monotonic. Timing must be interpreted in the original causal or
  per-agent order.

The inventory records, before compilation, the chosen trace hash, source
schema, timestamp sentinel rules, monotonicity, resolution, absent fields, and
the exact feature families enabled by that evidence. Missing or invalid timing
disables timing features; it is never synthesized. `seph-blog1` still cannot
test dwell, viewport, or focus-loss features because those observation streams
are absent. First-party Zine recording is required to validate those claims.

Episode identity is extent, not analysis:

```ts
type EpisodeExtentV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "episode-extent";
  trace: ZineId;
  fromTips: CausalTips;
  toTips: CausalTips;
  operationGroupIds: string[];
};

type EpisodeAnalysisV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "episode-analysis";
  episodeId: string;
  compilerVersion: string;
  materialCommitments: StreamCommitment[];
  requiredObservationCommitments: StreamCommitment[];
  revisionEdges: RevisionEdge[];
  deterministicFeatures: Record<string, number | string | boolean>;
};
```

`episodeId` hashes only canonical `EpisodeExtentV1`. Feature, linker, and
compiler changes produce a new analysis object naming the same episode. Frozen
manifests name both `episodeId` and the exact analysis and ranking versions.
This preserves cross-version ranking diffs without weakening exact prompt
reproduction.

Before seeing compiler or ranking output, freeze a preregistration artifact and
its SHA-256 containing:

- ten hand-labeled substantive replacement trajectories;
- ten hard negatives, including typo, whitespace, formatting, and initial bulk
  import windows;
- a human top-three ordering of consequential windows;
- trace hash, adapter version, label rubric, and enabled evidence fields.

Gate 1a passes only if:

1. every transaction reconstructs exactly and two runs produce byte-identical
   extent and analysis objects;
2. the linker recovers at least 8 of 10 substantive trajectories, and at least
   8 of its top 10 proposed links are substantive;
3. no high-confidence link crosses an unrelated structural section;
4. the top-ranked window is in the frozen human top three and is not a typo,
   whitespace-only change, formatting-only change, or the initial bulk import;
5. at least 7 of the top 10 ranked windows are frozen consequential labels and
   no more than 2 are frozen trivial labels.

Failure stops renderer and protocol work. Gate 1a does not choose the production
file reducer. A single-author trace cannot exercise the concurrency, merge,
memory, and offline-mobile workload that distinguishes eg-walker from a stable
sequential oplog.

### 12. Reply, Replay, and social identity

This section and the Press interface section record integrated product
consequences from the session. They are not part of the first reducer and
Episode IR experiment and cannot block or enlarge Gate 1.

A Reply is an independently owned signed trace object that may target:

- an exact Step;
- a span at an exact frontier;
- a revision episode or trajectory;
- a folder entry;
- a citation, attestation, or prior Reply.

Without a capability, an actionable proposed diff is still Reply content. The
target owner may adopt it locally with `derivedFrom` provenance.

Replay fetches exact committed ranges and keyframes asynchronously. Its base
interface is the text itself:

1. **Read:** encounter the current materialized text. This is the first Replay
   pass.
2. **Disclose:** open provenance, prior wording, voice, citation, or prompt
   context from a passage.
3. **Traverse:** step through operation groups, checkpoints, attention, sources,
   and revision trajectories.
4. **Guide:** optionally ask a model to identify or explain exact referenced
   parts.

The reader is not required to operate a player or consume a generated highlight
reel. Timeline controls, accelerated playback, and animation are optional
renderers over the same addressable trace.

```ts
type ReplayRef =
  | { zine: ZineId; step: StepRef }
  | { zine: ZineId; step: StepRef; span: { fromUtf16: number; toUtf16: number } }
  | { zine: ZineId; episode: string }
  | { zine: ZineId; trajectory: string }
  | { zine: ZineId; fromTips: CausalTips; toTips: CausalTips };
```

Optional model assistance has two forms:

1. **Pre-processing guide:** identify likely key parts, spell out why they may
   matter, and produce a reading list of exact `ReplayRef` links.
2. **Post-processing guide:** answer questions or explain patterns after the
   reader inspects all or part of the trace, again citing exact `ReplayRef`
   evidence.

Every guide stores its model receipt, Episode and ranking versions, selected
references, and rendered explanation. A guide is a derived annotation. It never
replaces, shortens, or changes the complete readable Replay. Accelerated playback
and animated transitions remain optional renderers over the same references.

Profile is a simple signed pointer to one already-published file or folder zine.
It does not follow private device focus and does not expose a live session.

Follows subscribe to published Profile or trace changes. Peers are transport or
trust relationships, not collaborators by implication. Correspondence is
derived from signed Reply, citation, and fork edges.

Nostr notes without trace provenance remain readable and replyable. A Zine Reply
may cite them as external objects without pretending they have Replay.
Attestation publishes both the Zine-native attestation and an additional kind-1
snapshot note referencing the exact zine and Step. Kind 1 remains a note, not a
coin.

### 14. Conflict and failure behavior

The product must make these states explicit:

- **Missing causal range:** show the Step but mark Replay unavailable or
  incomplete. Never fabricate a reconstruction.
- **Unauthorized operation:** retain it as an inspectable submission or Reply;
  do not include it in the accepted frontier automatically.
- **Revoked capability:** reject future automatic incorporation. Permit explicit
  local adoption with new provenance.
- **Concurrent same-span edits:** preserve both operation trajectories and show
  the conflict before or during checkpointing.
- **Missing voice descriptor:** show an unresolved voice id, signer, and evidence
  status rather than defaulting to the Step signer as producer.
- **Partial publication:** do not advertise Replay availability until promised
  keyframes and ranges are retrievable from at least one selected host.
- **Audience mismatch:** a Step commitment may exist without every observer
  receiving decryption keys. The interface must distinguish committed,
  obtainable, and decryptable.
- **Model receipt unavailable:** retain the local action and mark external
  corroboration absent. Do not invent a model signature.
- **Query or context over budget:** deterministically truncate by the visible
  salience order, preserve mandatory target context, and show exclusions.
- **Cycle in recursive folders:** stop traversal at the repeated identity and
  show the boundary.

### 15. Logical encoding and reducer contract

The types above describe the logical model. They are not permission to invent
implementation-specific hashes. The experiment uses one canonical fixture
encoding:

- UTF-8 canonical JSON with lexicographically sorted object keys;
- integers only for time, positions, counters, and lengths;
- array order retained where the schema declares order;
- absent optionals omitted rather than serialized as `null`;
- SHA-256 over canonical bytes for fixture and envelope identities;
- every hashed object begins with `schemaVersion` and `objectType`.

Production may later select a compact binary encoding, but it must reproduce the
same fixture identities or declare a new schema version.

For prototype objects:

```text
signatureDigest =
  SHA256("zine-sign-v1:" + objectType + NUL + canonical(unsignedBody))

signedObjectId =
  SHA256("zine-object-v1:" + objectType + NUL
         + canonical({ unsignedBody, signer, signature }))

unsignedObjectId =
  SHA256("zine-object-v1:" + objectType + NUL + canonical(body))
```

Domain strings are literal UTF-8. A signature covers `signatureDigest`.
References are exact ids:

```ts
type OpId = { groupId: string; index: number };
type CausalTips = OpId[]; // sorted, unique maximal operation ids

type ZineRef = { zineId: string; stepId: string };
type StepRef = string;       // signed Step object id
type CapabilityRef = string; // signed capability object id
type KeyframeRef = string;   // unsigned keyframe object id
type VoiceRef = string;      // voice genesis descriptor id

type SignedEnvelope<T> = {
  body: T;
  signer: Pubkey;
  signature: string;
};
```

The earlier `OperationGroupView` and `CapabilityView` are interface projections.
The body and envelope types in this section are the only canonical fixture
shapes.

Every referenced object has a canonical body:

```ts
type CapabilityBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "capability";
  issuer: Pubkey;
  subject: Pubkey;
  scope: ZineRef;
  recursive: boolean;
  rights: Array<"create" | "write" | "order" | "step" | "send" | "publish">;
  creationPolicy?: "inherit-governance" | "independent-child";
  issuedAtPolicy: { policySeq: number; policyHash: string };
  notAfterPolicySeq?: number;
};

type TraceHeadBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "trace-head";
  zineId: string;
  selectedStep: StepRef;
  selectedPolicy: { policySeq: number; policyHash: string };
  priorHead?: string;
};

type PolicyHeadBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "policy-head";
  owner: Pubkey;
  policySeq: number;
  policyHash: string;
  priorPolicyHead?: string;
};

type KeyframeBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "keyframe";
  zineId: string;
  tips: CausalTips;
  reducerVersion: string;
  stateHash: string;
  stateBytesHash: string;
};

type VoiceGenesisBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "voice-genesis";
  kind: "human" | "model" | "tool" | "composite";
  label: string;
  controller?: Pubkey;
  bindings?: Array<{
    pubkey: Pubkey;
    role: "speaker" | "controller" | "provider" | "witness";
  }>;
};
```

`voiceId` is the signed or unsigned object id of `VoiceGenesisBody`. A later
descriptor update is a separately signed object that names `voiceId`,
`priorDescriptor`, and the replacement metadata. It changes display metadata,
not historical contribution identity.

```ts
type ClosureManifest = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "closure-manifest";
  tips: CausalTips;
  groupIds: string[]; // sorted unique transitive closure
};

type OperationGroupBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "operation-group";
  target: ZineRef;
  baseStep: StepRef;
  parentTips: CausalTips;
  batches: VoiceBatch[];
  cause?: ReceiptRef;
};
```

`groupId` is the SHA-256 hash of the canonical group body. Each operation id is
`{groupId, index}` in flattened batch and operation order. Operations inside one
group execute in declared array order. Two deliveries with the same id and
different bytes are invalid. Duplicate delivery of identical bytes is a no-op.
`CausalTips` is an antichain of maximal parent operations, not the full history.
Its transitive closure is supplied and verified separately by
`ClosureManifest`. Applying a group removes parent tips that it causally covers
and adds the group's last operation as a new tip; a zero-operation group is
invalid.

The reducer rejects:

- a missing parent tip or incomplete transitive closure;
- a target or reducer kind mismatch;
- duplicate operation ids;
- an anchor not meaningful at the declared base;
- an operation group over 1 MiB of inserted or metadata bytes;
- more than 10,000 operations, 64 parent tips, or 64 voice batches in one group;
- invalid UTF-16 boundaries or malformed canonical encoding.

These are prototype ceilings. The fixture report must measure them before they
become protocol limits.

The reducer-neutral file fixture stores author intent:

```ts
type FileIntent =
  | { type: "insert"; atUtf16: number; text: string }
  | { type: "delete"; fromUtf16: number; toUtf16: number; expectedHash: string }
  | {
      type: "replace";
      fromUtf16: number;
      toUtf16: number;
      expectedHash: string;
      text: string;
    };
```

Every intent is interpreted against its exact `baseStep`. The sequential adapter
may run only fixtures with a total order. The identity-aware adapter receives the
same human intent plus the causal relationships needed to translate positions
into its native operations. The concurrency comparison uses a separate fixture
where both adapters receive two intents from one base; the sequential baseline
must report that it needs an external order rather than faking convergence.

Whichever file reducer is selected must pass:

1. delivery-order independence for a causally closed operation set;
2. deterministic concurrent-insert ordering by native operation identity;
3. deletion of the intended identity range rather than whichever text later
   occupies the same numeric offsets;
4. stable snapshot and visible provenance runs;
5. deterministic malformed-input rejection;
6. inversion or an explicit compensating operation for undo;
7. exact reconstruction at every fixture tip set.

### 16. Folder reducer invariants

Folder operation ids are idempotent and target stable `entryId` values.

The v1 reducer applies these rules:

- one accepted folder snapshot contains an `entryId` at most once;
- one direct child zine identity appears at most once;
- adding the same operation twice is a no-op;
- different concurrent adds for the same child produce an explicit membership
  conflict and cannot enter a clean Step until an authorized checkpoint resolves
  it;
- remove wins over a concurrent move or child-head advance for the same entry;
- moving or advancing an already removed entry is retained in Replay but has no
  material effect;
- `member.advance` accepts only a child Step that descends from the accepted
  child head;
- incomparable child heads produce a head conflict rather than an arbitrary
  lexicographic winner;
- concurrent custom-order moves produce an order conflict. An authorized
  `order` holder resolves it with `order.resolve`, naming the conflicting
  operation ids and issuing a new complete permutation;
- a complete custom permutation must contain every live direct entry exactly
  once and no removed or unknown entry;
- a folder Step that would create a recursive membership cycle is invalid;
- derived alphabetical, recency, citation, and activity sorts never emit folder
  operations.

The deterministic reducer may therefore return a state plus conflict set.
Convergence means every replica returns the same state and conflict set. It does
not mean the system silently makes an editorial choice.

### 17. Authority, submission, and Step state

Ownership is established by the signer of a zine's genesis Step. Transfer and
root-key recovery are explicitly outside v1. A new owner requires a new trace or
a later separately reviewed ownership protocol.

The owner signs a monotonically sequenced policy log containing capability grants
and revocations. Verification of any delegated action walks:

1. genesis owner;
2. selected policy head and active owner-issued grant;
3. submission or Step signer;
4. exact target scope and requested right.

A contributor submission is:

```ts
type SubmissionBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "submission";
  target: ZineRef;
  baseStep: StepRef;
  operationGroupsRoot: string;
  operationGroupCount: number;
  capabilityId: string;
  nonce: string;
};
```

`SubmissionBody` is carried in `SignedEnvelope<SubmissionBody>`. `nonce` is 128
random bits. The signed object id is the submission id. Repeated ids are
idempotent; the same signer and nonce with different bytes are invalid.

The submitted operation groups are topologically sorted by causal dependency,
with unrelated groups ordered by `groupId` ascending. Leaves are:

```text
leaf = SHA256(0x00 || canonical(OperationGroupBody))
node = SHA256(0x01 || leftHash || rightHash)
emptyRoot = SHA256(0x02)
```

The tree recursively splits a non-power-of-two list at the largest power of two
smaller than its length. `operationGroupCount` must equal the number of leaves,
and `operationGroupsRoot` must equal the resulting root. The fixture suite
contains empty, one-leaf, two-leaf, three-leaf, duplicate-delivery, wrong-count,
wrong-order, and inclusion-proof vectors. An empty submission is invalid even
though the tree function defines `emptyRoot`.

A Step contains:

```ts
type StepCheckpointBody = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "step-checkpoint";
  zine: ZineId;
  checkpointParents: StepRef[];
  acceptedTips: CausalTips;
  snapshotHash: string;
  streamCommitments: StreamCommitment[];
  keyframe?: KeyframeRef;
  policy: { policySeq: number; policyHash: string };
  capability?: CapabilityRef;
};
```

`StepCheckpointBody` is carried by `SignedEnvelope<StepCheckpointBody>`. One
signer may create a linear history. Delegated signers may create concurrent
checkpoint branches. A `TraceHead` selection, signed by the owner or a holder of
the root owner key, names the canonical head. A resolving Step may name several
checkpoint parents and must prove that its accepted operation tips contain
the chosen material from each parent. The UI calls this **incorporation**, not
merge, and shows any omitted branch material.

Head selection never discards a branch or its signatures. It only selects the
default projection. A Profile can point only to a sent or published selected
head.

### 18. Capture and disclosure lifecycle

Material and performance data have different lifecycle rules:

| Stream | Local capture | Step inclusion | Send/Publish | Retention and deletion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Material operations | Lossless while editing | All operations causally required by the snapshot | Same access class as the snapshot | Uncommitted draft may be abandoned; a committed prefix remains immutable |
| Keystroke timing | Lossless monotonic deltas while recording | Included recording segments | Audience and keys selected separately | Uncommitted segment may be deleted; committed ciphertext may remain even if keys are later destroyed |
| Selection and viewport | Event-driven, viewport coalesced to at most 10 Hz | Included recording segments | Audience and keys selected separately | Same as timing |
| Focus, tabs, navigation | Lossless state changes | Included recording segments | Audience and keys selected separately | Same as timing |
| Queries and model receipts | Lossless for executed actions | Required when they caused included material | Evidence audience cannot be broader by accident than the material audience | Local unused queries follow local retention policy |
| Voice and capability objects | On creation or receipt | Exact versions referenced by the Step | Delivered wherever needed for verification | Signed history is retained with its referencing Step |

Recording is visible per zine. Material recording is required for traced
authoring. Performance recording defaults on after the user has enabled it for
that zine and always shows an active indicator.

Shield does not create unverifiable holes inside one committed prefix. It closes
the current performance segment and opens a new excluded segment. Step chooses
whole performance segments. A pre-Step inspector shows duration, event classes,
audience, and approximate disclosure size for every included segment.
By default, Step includes all unshielded performance segments completed since
the prior Step. The author may exclude a whole segment before signing; exclusion
never changes the material snapshot.

Unpublished local performance segments may be deleted. A signed Step cannot be
edited to remove a committed segment. A later removal request may affect relay
retention but cannot make recipients forget bytes or keys they already received.

Prototype resource targets:

- material operations are never dropped;
- selection and viewport capture is event-driven and coalesced to 10 Hz;
- focus, tab, query, and command transitions are lossless;
- compressed process storage stays below 5 MiB per active editing hour on the
  public trace fixtures;
- recorder overhead stays below 2 ms p95 per editor transaction on desktop;
- the mobile simulation measures fewer than 36,000 coalesced observation events,
  less than 5 MiB local growth, less than 60 seconds aggregate recorder CPU time,
  and no background network wakeups per scripted active hour;
- backpressure drops intermediate viewport samples first and writes an explicit
  observation-gap marker;
- keyframe seek targets are 250 ms p95 desktop and 500 ms p95 mobile for the
  fixture corpus.

Battery acceptance is deferred to representative physical devices after the
simulation passes. Run the same scripted 60-minute editing workload three times
with recording disabled and three times enabled on at least one current iOS and
one current Android device at matched battery health and screen settings. The
median incremental drain target is at most 3 percentage points and no run may
exceed 5 points. These are experiment gates, not protocol promises.

### 19. Episode IR and ranking contract

```ts
type EpisodeExtentV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "episode-extent";
  trace: ZineId;
  fromTips: CausalTips;
  toTips: CausalTips;
  operationGroupIds: string[];
};

type EpisodeAnalysisV1 = {
  schemaVersion: 1;
  objectType: "episode-analysis";
  episodeId: string;
  compilerVersion: string;
  materialCommitments: StreamCommitment[];
  requiredObservationCommitments: StreamCommitment[];
  revisionEdges: RevisionEdge[];
  deterministicFeatures: Record<string, number | string | boolean>;
};
```

`episodeId` is the hash of canonical `EpisodeExtentV1`: trace, causal bounds,
and included operation-group identities. It does not change when a compiler,
feature, or linker version changes. Exact commitments, revision edges, and
features live in a separately hashed `EpisodeAnalysisV1`; a frozen manifest
names the exact analysis and ranking versions used.

Optional observations used only for animation or display live in a separate
`EpisodePresentation` object and cannot affect identity, deterministic features,
ranking, or prompt selection. Model-derived semantic distance lives in a
versioned `EpisodeSemanticAnnotation` naming the exact episode, model, inputs,
and score; it is never embedded in `RevisionEdgeV1`.

The structure-only v1 deterministic ranker may consume:

- sustained material churn;
- delete and replacement trajectories;
- accumulated and net revision distance;
- cuts late in the causal composition order;
- voice transitions;
- exact citation and query transitions.

When the inventory validates recorded wall-clock timing, a named
timing-qualified ranking version may additionally consume pause-then-burst,
session boundaries, and edit velocity. Dwell, viewport, and focus-loss features
require their own recorded observation streams. An unavailable feature is
absent, not zero, and rankings with different evidence profiles are not compared
as though they used the same inputs.

Each feature has an integer or fixed-point value. Ranking version and weights are
named in the manifest. Equal scores break by `episodeId` ascending.
Model-derived semantic distance is a versioned annotation and cannot affect v1
identity or deterministic rank unless a later ranking version explicitly names
the annotation schema, model, and score.

The invariant is:

> Direct Replay begins with ordinary reading of the complete verified text and
> keeps the complete trace addressable without ranking. Prompt assembly and
> optional Replay guides consume the same Episode IR and ranking version. Prompt
> assembly declares a scope filter and byte budget. A Replay guide can reproduce
> the exact selected order from the frozen manifest and link every item back into
> direct Replay.

If mandatory current-target content exceeds the model window, the manager first
chunks it deterministically on document structure while retaining the exact
target range. If the smallest permitted target chunk plus required receipt
metadata still exceeds the window, the request fails before contacting the
model. Mandatory context is never silently truncated.

### 20. Sparse Replay bundle

A minimal Replay bundle contains:

1. the selected signed Step, selected `TraceHead`, genesis Step or configured
   trusted trace anchor, and the minimal checkpoint ancestry connecting them;
2. every referenced `StreamCommitment`;
3. a keyframe whose state hash and frontier are committed by the Step or an
   included ancestor, plus that keyframe-committing Step;
4. Merkle proofs and bytes for every operation and observation range from that
   keyframe to the episode frontier;
5. a sorted causal-closure manifest across all involved feeds;
6. reducer, schema, episode-compiler, and ranking versions;
7. the selected `PolicyHead`, every policy record needed to reach it, every
   referenced capability, and the authorization proofs for each Step and
   submission;
8. exact voice descriptors and evidence receipts referenced by the selected
   ranges;
9. all material and required observation commitments named by the Episode.

The verifier:

1. verifies owner, capability, Step, submission, and feed signatures;
2. verifies each feed root and range proof at the committed length;
3. checks the causal closure has no missing parents;
4. validates the keyframe hash and frontier;
5. applies the reducer to the requested frontier;
6. checks the resulting snapshot hash against the Step;
7. recomputes Episode IR and deterministic ranking;
8. fails reproducibility when any material or ranking-required observation is
   unavailable;
9. marks only presentation-optional observations as absent rather than
   synthesizing them.

Multi-feed commitments are sorted by `(streamId, coreKey)` before they enter a
Step hash. Sparse verification proves completeness through the named Step. It
does not prove that no newer unpublished Step exists.

### 21. Experiment gates and evidence labeling

Public datasets contain different evidence profiles. Every imported field is
labeled `recorded`, `derived`, `sentinel`, `invalid`, or `synthetic`. Synthetic
evidence may exercise a renderer but cannot count toward an empirical salience
result. Timestamp validity is inventory item zero, as specified in §11A.

The work proceeds through independent gates:

1. **Gate 1a — single-trace reduction:** run the frozen §11A preregistration on
   `seph-blog1`.
2. **Gate 1b — evidence breadth:** inventory a second, structure-only trace and
   test the remaining trajectory classes without timing claims.
3. **Gate 2 — guide parity:** render the trace as a complete readable Replay,
   beginning with ordinary text reading; render one Episode selection as prompt
   cards and an optional Replay guide, then reproduce the prompt selection as
   exact links into direct Replay.
4. **Gate 3 — reducer adapter parity:** encode the sequential evidence in the
   simple oplog and identity-aware candidates. This gate validates adapters and
   measurements but cannot choose the production reducer.
5. **Gate 4 — representative concurrent construction:** run same-file,
   same-paragraph, multi-voice, offline desktop/mobile, and delayed-merge
   workloads. Choose the reducer only here, using convergence, provenance,
   conflict legibility, load time, merge cost, and memory.
6. **Gate 5 — folder and capability simulation:** validate grants, revocation,
   duplicate delivery, concurrent membership, and delegated checkpoint
   branches.
7. **Gate 6 — sparse proof bundle:** reconstruct an episode from a keyframe and
   verified ranges.
8. **Gate 7 — protocol proposal:** only passing behavior becomes a wire-format
   change.

Failure at one gate does not silently expand or invalidate an earlier gate.

## Success Criteria

Each gate has its own stop condition.

**Gate 1: Recorded-trace reduction**

- Complete Gate 1a on `seph-blog1` under the frozen §11A thresholds, then
  inventory a second public trace and label every input field by evidence class.
- Reconstruct every available source snapshot exactly.
- Emit stable extent ids twice from identical inputs and retain those ids across
  feature-version changes.
- Identify hand-labeled replacement, movement, restoration, and terminal-removal
  examples without synthetic observation evidence.

**Gate 2: Direct Replay plus shared guidance**

- Render the current materialized text as the first Replay pass without model
  output, with the complete trace progressively addressable from it.
- Render the same ordered Episode ids as prompt-context cards and an optional
  pre-reading guide.
- Reproduce the exact prompt selection as `ReplayRef` links from its frozen
  manifest.
- Compare deletion-aware selection with latest-only and random windows under one
  byte budget.
- Display a verified "this revives prior wording" result with exact source
  ranges.

**Gate 3: Reducer adapter parity**

- Compare the sequential baseline and identity-aware reducer on identical
  sequential inputs and produce comparable measurements.
- Do not select the production reducer from this gate.

**Gate 4: Representative concurrent construction**

- Compare the sequential baseline and identity-aware candidates without
  pretending the baseline resolves unordered concurrent intent.
- Preserve two interleaved voices and the provenance of surviving and deleted
  text.
- Simulate desktop and mobile editing the same paragraph from one frontier and
  preserve both causal histories and deterministic conflicts.
- Select the production reducer only from this workload.

**Gate 5: Folder authority**

- Validate create, write, order, step, send, and publish rights independently,
  plus owner-only canonical head selection.
- Reject a non-owner capability issuer, widened recursive scope, revoked
  capability, duplicate submission, invalid child advance, duplicate direct
  child, and recursive cycle fixtures.
- Demonstrate a Step created offline whose id remains unchanged through Send and
  Publish retries.

**Gate 6: Sparse verification**

- Replay from a nearby keyframe and verified ranges without reading the full
  history.
- Detect a missing causal parent, invalid feed proof, wrong snapshot hash, and
  unavailable optional observation range.
- Meet the provisional desktop, mobile, storage, and recorder budgets.

No gate requires a live network dependency. Protocol work begins only after the
behavior it would standardize passes its gate.
