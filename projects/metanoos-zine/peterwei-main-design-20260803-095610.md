## Problem Statement

Zine has substantial prior product and protocol design, but no direct demand evidence for a commercial wedge. The startup hypothesis is not that writing provenance is generally valuable. It is narrower:

> When AI is allowed in a paid writing audition, a reviewer may learn something consequential about the candidate's judgment from sources, model use, revisions, rejected directions, and acceptance decisions that the finished sample and current assessment process do not reveal.

The hypothesis remains unverified. Current content organizations already use applications, portfolios, assessments, samples, rubrics, and trial projects. Zine has no wedge unless an optional process-evidence packet changes a shadow evaluation enough to justify the extra candidate and reviewer burden. The first pilot cannot affect the candidate's live hiring outcome.

The Git history and GitHub remote have no commits. The local working tree does contain an uncommitted clean-room implementation scaffold across desktop, web, reader, mobile, and core packages. That code is evidence of builder effort, not demand, and this office-hours design neither evaluates nor authorizes shipping it. Any reuse or continuation requires a separately reviewed implementation plan after the discovery gate.

## Demand Evidence

### Direct evidence

None.

- No buyer has paid, committed an upcoming audition, changed a hiring workflow, or said that missing process evidence caused a costly mistake.
- No candidate has completed an uncoached Zine audition for a real reviewer.
- No reviewer has scored the same work before and after inspecting a Zine process packet.
- Existing implementation history proves builder agency, not demand.

### Current category evidence

The market confirms that writer evaluation is real and structured, but it does not confirm that process replay adds value:

- [Compose.ly](https://www.compose.ly/faq) says applicants pass a two-part assessment, submit a resume, portfolio, LinkedIn profile, and three samples, then complete multiple trial projects. It maintains hundreds of writers and editors who are rated after completed projects.
- [Ashley Strosnider](https://www.compose.ly/author/ashley-strosnider) is publicly identified as Compose.ly's Head of Editorial, with responsibility for team leadership, process implementation, vendor management, and editorial judgment.
- [Draft.dev](https://draft.dev/about) coordinates 300+ vetted engineer-writers, uses AI-assisted research and drafting with human developer and editor review, and publishes guidance recommending rubrics, samples, and paid test assignments.
- [Workello](https://workello.com/seo-writer-test/) is direct competitive truth: it sells writer-testing workflows, supports paid or unpaid assessments, and reports more than 10,000 candidates tested. Its assessment was created by Gordana Sretenovic after scaling a writing and editing team to 45 people.

This evidence raises the bar. Zine must not claim that existing assessments are weak merely because they do not preserve a composition trace.

## Status Quo

The current evaluation protocol is the real competitor, not Google Docs:

1. Recruit from a marketplace, referral network, or application form.
2. Filter using a resume, portfolio, LinkedIn profile, and prior samples.
3. Administer a structured assessment or paid trial assignment.
4. Score the finished work against a rubric for accuracy, clarity, audience fit, research, and technical or subject-matter quality.
5. Interview the candidate about choices that remain unclear.
6. Begin with limited paid work and rate performance over subsequent projects.

AI is already entering production workflows. Draft.dev publicly describes AI-assisted research and drafting with human expert review, while Compose.ly offers separate AI-assisted content services. The first-pilot question is not whether AI is present or whether Zine improves prediction. It is only whether inspectable use of AI and revision evidence changes a reviewer's evaluation at acceptable burden. Improved prediction of editorial judgment, reliability, or post-hire performance requires later outcome research and is not established by a mechanism pass.

### Narrowest wedge

One optional process-evidence packet evaluated after one real, compensated, AI-permitted writing audition:

1. The organization supplies the brief, evaluation rubric, and normal candidate protections.
2. The organization compensates the candidate at its normal trial rate. Founder-funded candidate compensation does not count as buyer commitment.
3. The organization completes and locks its live hiring decision through its normal process. The process packet cannot affect that decision.
4. With explicit candidate consent, an explicit-export packet reveals only allowed sources, selected AI conversation exports, document revision exports, candidate explanations, item provenance, and known evidence gaps.
5. After the live decision is final, a separate research reviewer with no current or future authority over the candidate locks their own finished-sample baseline, inspects the packet, and records within-reviewer changes to rubric score, counterfactual advance or reject decision, required interview question, confidence score, or nothing. The research reviewer remains blinded to the live score and decision until their post-packet evaluation is locked.

Zine does not replace the assessment, score candidates automatically, detect AI, prove identity, or claim that a trace reveals intent.

## Constraints

- No new product feature is authorized before the discovery gate passes.
- Contentellect remains excluded on trust grounds.
- The first 20 minutes of each interview reconstruct a recent real evaluation without showing Zine or asking leading questions about provenance.
- The evidence codebook is locked before the first interview. Interview compliments, feature suggestions, abstract AI concern, and willingness to see a demo are not demand.
- The audition must provide **candidate compensation** at the organization's normal trial rate. A **buyer pilot fee** paid to Zine is optional for the first mechanism test. **Buyer operational commitment** means a named organization, decision owner, reviewer, event, and schedule confirmed in writing.
- Participation is optional. The organization's live hiring decision is completed before packet review, and the separate research reviewer has no current or future authority over the candidate. Live hiring staff do not receive the identifiable packet or research result, so participation cannot affect the candidate's live opportunity or a later assignment, renewal, or reputation decision.
- Candidate consent, reviewer scope, retention, deletion, and withdrawal terms are explicit before capture.
- The packet contains only deliberately shared logical evidence. No keystroke timing, screen recording, raw cursor movement, hidden external-tool monitoring, or inference about attention and intent is permitted.
- The reviewer locks the live finished-sample score and decision before receiving the packet. All later changes are shadow research outcomes only.
- A failed Zine workflow cannot count against a candidate.
- The first pilot uses an explicit-export manual packet only. Build recovery and product use are deferred until a mechanism pass and a separately approved implementation plan.
- The uncommitted local implementation has not been evaluated in this session and cannot be cited as a validated capability or demand signal.
- The first pilot is limited to a United States independent-contractor writing audition with an adult candidate, no regulated-sector role, and no live client-confidential material. A different jurisdiction or worker classification requires a new legal review before scheduling.

## Premises

1. Zine's first startup question is whether process evidence changes an AI-permitted writing evaluation beyond the finished sample and existing assessment at acceptable burden. Predictive improvement is a later hypothesis.
2. The pain remains unverified. Current agencies already use serious tests and trials, so doing nothing may be acceptable.
3. Zine enters as an optional shadow-evaluation packet after an existing compensated trial, not as a hiring platform or mandatory candidate environment.
4. Success uses one outcome matrix. A live hiring decision never changes during the first pilot; only the recorded shadow evaluation can change.
5. Ashley Strosnider, Karl Hughes, and Gordana Sretenovic are preferred leads for three distinct interview roles. Role-matched substitutions are allowed under the rules below.
6. Product work remains gated until at least two interviewees independently name the same costly uncertainty and one buyer offers a real upcoming pilot.
7. Prior concepts and the uncommitted local implementation remain design context only. Reuse is not assumed and requires a separately reviewed implementation plan.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Interview-only kill test

Run the three interviews with one locked protocol and no early demo.

- Effort: small, 8–12 active human hours over 2–4 weeks; about 1 hour of CC+gstack support.
- Risk: medium.
- Completeness: 6/10.
- Advantage: fastest path to falsifying the pain without candidate or privacy burden.
- Limitation: cannot prove adoption or decision impact and may produce polite false positives.

### Approach B: Interviews followed by one concierge pilot

Run the three interviews, apply a written gate, then conduct one candidate-compensated audition with a post-decision shadow packet review if and only if the gate passes.

- Effort: medium, 20–35 active human hours over roughly 8–14 weeks: discovery 3–5 weeks, readiness 1–2 weeks, and up to 8 weeks for a valid hiring event; about 3 hours of CC+gstack support.
- Risk: medium.
- Completeness: 10/10.
- Advantage: tests pain, behavioral commitment, candidate burden, reviewer burden, and decision impact.
- Limitation: scheduling, consent, evidence handling, and pilot readiness can fail independently of the product thesis.

### Approach C: Counterfactual reviewer study

Have reviewers score consented historical or purpose-made samples, reveal manually assembled process packets, then score again.

- Effort: small to medium, 10–16 human hours over 2–4 weeks; about 2 hours of CC+gstack support.
- Risk: high.
- Completeness: 7/10.
- Advantage: quickly tests whether process evidence can change judgment.
- Limitation: constructed material can manufacture the result and does not test adoption, candidate consent, or willingness to pay.

## Recommended Approach

Use **Approach B: interviews followed by one concierge pilot**.

### Stage 0: Prepare the evidence protocol

Before contacting anyone, Peter locks the glossary, codebook, script, and outcome matrix below. A second coder who has not seen the Zine pitch reviews interview evidence before the discovery gate is scored. Borderline cases resolve against passing the gate.

#### Operational glossary

| Term | Locked definition |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified interview | A person who directly owned or materially influenced at least one writer assessment in the prior 12 months and can reconstruct one case from intake through post-hire or rejection outcome. |
| Independent account | Elicited before the Zine concept is described, with no coordination between interviewees and no disclosure of another interviewee's answer. |
| Same uncertainty | Two accounts map to the same predeclared code and match the code's inclusion example; thematic resemblance decided after the fact is insufficient. |
| Concrete cost | A quantified time, money, missed-deadline, client-escalation, replacement, or foregone-candidate consequence. |
| Material uncertainty | The uncertainty caused at least two person-hours of rework, one missed deadline or client escalation, a candidate replacement, or a changed hiring decision. |
| Consequential shadow change | At least a one-point change on a five-point rubric dimension, a counterfactual advance/reject flip, a newly required interview question that must be answered before the counterfactual decision can proceed, or at least a two-point confidence change on a ten-point scale. |
| Buyer operational commitment | Written confirmation of the organization, decision owner, reviewer, compensated audition, candidate route, and target schedule. This is distinct from a buyer pilot fee. |
| Acceptable candidate burden | At most 30 additional minutes for consent, export, redaction, and confirmation; no high-severity privacy incident; candidate says they would repeat. |
| Acceptable reviewer burden | At most 20 minutes to inspect the packet and record the shadow evaluation; reviewer says they would repeat. |

The codebook covers quality prediction, AI/tool judgment, source reliability, revision discipline, identity or outsourcing, candidate burden, and reviewer burden. Each code contains inclusion, exclusion, and counterexample text. Consented recordings are preferred. If recording is declined, Peter takes near-verbatim notes and asks the interviewee to confirm the factual workflow summary. The second coder receives only a redacted transcript of minutes 0–20, the locked codebook, and no interview identity, post-concept reaction, first-coder result, or gate status. The second coder locks their codes before any comparison. Disagreement and uncoded counterevidence remain in the record; Peter cannot erase them to manufacture convergence.

### Stage 1: Three interviews

Interview the three roles, preferring Ashley, Karl, then Gordana.

#### Timed 30-minute script

- **0:00–2:00, permission and scope:** Ask to record. State that this is research about writer evaluation, not a sales call, and that no candidate or client details are required.
- **2:00–7:00, latest case:** “Tell me about the most recent writer you evaluated, starting when the need appeared.” Required: role, intake route, interviewer responsibility, and outcome.
- **7:00–13:00, current protocol:** Required: filters, samples, rubric, assessment or compensated trial, AI policy, and who made the decision.
- **13:00–18:00, failure and cost:** Required: what the finished work could not reveal, one later surprise or miss, and quantified consequence. Anti-leading probes: “What happened next?” “How much time did that add?” “Did it alter the decision?”
- **18:00–20:00, recurrence:** Required: whether the uncertainty has recurred and whether a similar compensated evaluation is upcoming.
- **20:00–22:00, neutral concept statement:** “We are studying whether a candidate-controlled packet of explicitly shared sources, selected AI interactions, document revisions, and explanations would answer any question your current assessment leaves open. It would not detect AI, prove identity, record screens or keystrokes, or change the first candidate's live outcome.”
- **22:00–27:00, mechanism test:** Ask what question the packet would answer, which rubric or decision it could change, which fields are unnecessary or unsafe, and the maximum acceptable candidate and reviewer burden.
- **27:00–30:00, behavior:** Ask about an upcoming event, named reviewer, operational commitment, referral, and conditions for a shadow pilot.

If the interviewee cannot reconstruct a real evaluation by minute 10, stop the product section, ask for a referral, and classify the interview as unqualified. Optional prompts may be skipped for time; required fields may not. An incomplete required field prevents the interview from counting toward the discovery gate.

### Discovery gate

Proceed toward a pilot only if all conditions hold:

- all three interviews are complete;
- at least two interviewees independently describe the same predeclared material uncertainty;
- each repeated uncertainty meets the locked material-uncertainty and concrete-cost definitions;
- the second coder agrees that the accounts meet the code's inclusion rule;
- at least one buyer-side organization has an upcoming candidate-compensated, AI-permitted audition;
- that organization makes a buyer operational commitment; and
- the desired value is evaluating judgment, not covert detection, surveillance, or identity proof.

If three qualified role interviews complete but the evidence conditions fail, stop or change the wedge. If a qualified role cannot be reached under the timebox and substitution rule, stop execution as **inconclusive access** without updating the demand hypothesis. Do not add features to compensate for missing demand.

### Stage 2: Pilot-readiness gate

Before involving a candidate:

- name the buyer, decision owner, live reviewer, separate research reviewer, candidate route, and data custodian; document that the research reviewer has no current or future authority over the candidate and cannot disclose the identifiable packet or result to live hiring staff;
- confirm a United States adult independent-contractor writing audition with no regulated role or live client-confidential material;
- obtain written approval from the organization's HR/legal owner and Peter's named qualified privacy or employment reviewer;
- document candidate compensation, consent, normal-path alternative, accommodations, evidence scope, withdrawal, retention, deletion, and incident terms;
- define the live rubric and lock the live score and decision before any packet is revealed;
- define the smallest explicit-export packet, provenance for each item, and every evidence gap;
- lock and sign the packet-validity specification before the live event, then complete its artifact checklist before transfer;
- rehearse the manual export, candidate redaction, encrypted transfer, reviewer access, shadow scoring, access log, and verified deletion with synthetic material; and
- confirm that the first pilot is a mechanism test and does not validate Zine product usability.

If those conditions are not met, the pilot does not run.

#### First-pilot packet and data handling

The packet may contain only:

- the final compensated-trial sample;
- source URLs or candidate-provided source files that the candidate is authorized to share;
- candidate-selected AI conversation exports, redacted before transfer;
- document revision-history exports or diffs that the source tool can demonstrably produce;
- a candidate-written explanation of substantive choices; and
- a manifest recording item type, origin, export time, candidate attestation, redactions, and gaps.

The signed packet-validity specification requires:

1. final compensated-trial sample and locked live decision;
2. candidate consent, attestation, and complete item manifest;
3. candidate-authorized source list;
4. at least one demonstrable document revision export or diff;
5. at least one relevant candidate-selected AI conversation export when AI was used;
6. candidate explanation of substantive choices and declared evidence gaps; and
7. the research reviewer's independently locked finished-sample rubric and confidence score before packet access.

Permissible gaps include unavailable rejected alternatives, exact action timing, omitted unrelated conversation, and external-tool actions that cannot be exported. If the candidate does not use AI, or if consent, final sample, revision evidence, applicable AI evidence, manifest, live lock, or research-reviewer baseline is missing, the promised mechanism was not completed: classify the case as **Fail**, not Inconclusive. Unsafe redaction and an explicit-export incompatibility are also mechanism failures. Candidate, data custodian, and research reviewer sign the validity specification before the event; the custodian verifies and signs the completed artifact checklist before transfer.

The packet must not contain credentials, tokens, hidden telemetry, screen recordings, keystroke or cursor data, unrelated conversations, full account metadata, unnecessary personal information, client-confidential material, regulated data, or copyrighted full text the candidate cannot share. Rejected alternatives and acceptance decisions appear only when an explicit export demonstrates them or the candidate describes them as an attested explanation. Completeness and truth remain unknown and are never implied.

Peter is the pilot data custodian. The named research reviewer is the only packet recipient. That reviewer must be contractually firewalled from any current or future decision about the candidate, remain blinded to the live score and decision until the post-packet evaluation is locked, and return only de-identified aggregate findings to live hiring staff. Transfer uses an encrypted channel; storage uses an encrypted local volume; each access is logged by person and time. No packet data enters analytics, model training, a relay, a public repository, or marketing. Default deletion is 30 days after the shadow review, followed by written deletion confirmation from custodian and reviewer.

On suspected exposure, access is revoked, the pilot stops, and the candidate and organization are notified within 24 hours with known scope and containment steps. Applicable legal notice rules override that operational target.

#### Withdrawal semantics

| Withdrawal point | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Before capture | Collect nothing; normal audition continues. If withdrawal is caused by the protocol's privacy or burden concerns, classify the mechanism **Fail**; if a documented reason is unrelated to the protocol, classify it Inconclusive. |
| After capture but before transfer | Delete exports and manifest; no packet is shared. Protocol-caused withdrawal is **Fail**; a documented unrelated reason is Inconclusive. |
| After transfer but before reviewer access | Revoke access and obtain deletion confirmation; no shadow review occurs. Protocol-caused withdrawal is **Fail**; a documented unrelated reason is Inconclusive. |
| After reviewer access | Human knowledge cannot be deleted. Delete artifacts and retain only a withdrawal audit record. Protocol-caused withdrawal is **Fail**; only a documented unrelated reason is Inconclusive. The live decision is already final and remains unaffected. |
| After aggregate reporting | Remove the case from future analysis where feasible. No identifiable reporting is allowed without separate written consent. Classify the outcome using the same cause rule. |

### Stage 3: One concierge pilot

1. The organization runs its normal candidate-compensated trial with AI explicitly permitted.
2. The candidate uses explicit exports, redacts them, attests the manifest, and sees the exact packet before transfer.
3. The organization locks its live score and hiring decision without packet access.
4. A separate research reviewer with no current or future authority over the candidate independently locks their own rubric, counterfactual decision, required questions, and confidence score from the finished sample alone, while blinded to the live score and decision.
5. Only after the live and research locks does the research reviewer inspect the packet and repeat the same shadow evaluation. Impact is measured within that reviewer's own pre/post scores, never by comparing two different reviewers. The live score and decision are unblinded to the research reviewer only after the post-packet evaluation is locked; live hiring staff never receive the identifiable packet or case result.
6. The reviewer records every shadow score, counterfactual decision, question, and confidence change, including no change.
7. The candidate and reviewer separately report setup time, confusion, privacy discomfort, and whether they would repeat the workflow.

### Authoritative outcome matrix

First determine why the event could not complete. Only a documented exogenous interruption—candidate illness, an unrelated cancellation, or verified infrastructure corruption—is **Inconclusive**. A breakdown caused by this protocol, including privacy or burden withdrawal, unsafe redaction, export incompatibility, legal rejection of the mechanism, or early packet exposure, is **Fail**. For completed cases, classify **Fail** for prohibited use, unacceptable burden, or a valid no-change result; classify **Mechanism pass** only when every pass condition holds; otherwise classify a valid subthreshold signal as **Supporting**.

| Outcome | Required evidence | Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery pass | Three qualified role interviews; two independently coded accounts of the same material uncertainty; second-coder agreement; one buyer operational commitment. | Pilot-readiness work only. |
| Mechanism pass | A valid within-reviewer shadow review produces at least one consequential shadow change; candidate and reviewer burdens are within limits; both say they would repeat; buyer requests a second test in writing within 30 days. | A separately reviewed implementation or repeat-pilot plan. It does not authorize product work by itself. |
| Supporting | Valid packet produces only an optional follow-up question, a one-point confidence change on a ten-point scale, or a consequential threshold change without buyer repeat commitment, while burden and use remain acceptable. | Refine and run at most one predeclared follow-up mechanism test. |
| Fail | Valid packet produces no threshold or subthreshold change; burden exceeds either limit; candidate or reviewer would not repeat; desired use is detection, surveillance, or identity proof; or the protocol causes withdrawal, unsafe redaction, missing core evidence, export incompatibility, legal rejection, or early exposure. Fail takes precedence over Supporting even if a score changed. | Stop or change the wedge. |
| Inconclusive | A documented exogenous candidate illness, unrelated hiring cancellation, unavailable event, or verified infrastructure corruption prevents completion. | At most one role-matched replacement event; never reinterpret as pass or fail. |

#### Invalidation and retry policy

| Event | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate withdraws or drops before transfer | If the documented reason is unrelated to the protocol, mark Inconclusive and allow one replacement event. If privacy, burden, consent, or another protocol condition caused it, mark **Fail** and stop. |
| Hiring event is canceled or no event is available within eight weeks of discovery pass | If cancellation is unrelated to the protocol, mark Inconclusive access, not demand failure, and allow one replacement event. Protocol-caused cancellation is **Fail**. |
| Reviewer changes before live lock | Restart readiness with the new reviewer. |
| Packet is exposed before the live decision and research baseline locks | Mark **Fail**; do not retry with that candidate. |
| Locked score is edited after exposure | Mark **Fail**. |
| Core export is absent or candidate redaction cannot make it safe | Mark **Fail**; never reconstruct or infer the missing evidence. |
| Transfer or viewing tool fails before exposure | If verified infrastructure corruption caused the failure, permit one technical retry with the identical packet and checksum. If the promised export or viewing workflow is incompatible, mark **Fail**. |
| Tool fails after partial reviewer exposure | Mark **Fail**; no selective restart. |
| Required HR, legal, privacy, or employment approval rejects the mechanism | Mark **Fail**. A legal rejection unrelated to this mechanism is Inconclusive only when documented. |

Across the first pilot cycle, allow at most one replacement hiring event for a documented exogenous interruption and one technical retry for verified infrastructure corruption. A protocol-caused failure never earns a replacement or retry. Extra retries risk selecting only a favorable result.

One mechanism pass shows only that the packet can change reviewer evaluation at acceptable burden. It does not show that the change is more accurate or predicts post-hire performance, and it does not prove product-market fit. It grants permission to repeat the experiment and consider a separately reviewed implementation plan.

## Success Criteria

### Discovery

- Three qualified role interviews, preferring Ashley, Karl, and Gordana and using at most one role-matched substitute per unavailable lead.
- Every interview reconstructs a real recent evaluation before any Zine explanation.
- Two interviewees independently map to the same predeclared material-uncertainty code and concrete consequence.
- A blinded second coder agrees with the inclusion decision and all counterevidence remains visible.
- At least one buyer-side organization makes a buyer operational commitment for a candidate-compensated, AI-permitted audition.

### Pilot integrity

- The first event is a United States adult independent-contractor writing audition with required legal/privacy approvals.
- The candidate gives informed consent and the organization completes the live hiring decision without packet access.
- The explicit-export packet contains only allowed fields, item provenance, candidate redactions, attestations, and gap labels.
- The research reviewer is separate, has no current or future authority over the candidate, remains blinded to the live score and decision until their post-packet evaluation is locked, and cannot disclose identifiable packet data or results to live hiring staff.
- The live score and decision are locked, and the research reviewer locks their own finished-sample baseline, before the research reviewer receives the packet.
- Every shadow change and no-change is recorded against the same rubric and threshold definitions.
- Withdrawal, access logging, encrypted transfer and storage, incident handling, and verified deletion follow the written protocol.
- Pilot or export failure cannot reduce the candidate's live score or opportunity.

### Behavioral evidence

- A mechanism pass requires at least one consequential shadow change at a locked threshold.
- Candidate burden is no more than 30 additional minutes and reviewer burden is no more than 20 minutes.
- Candidate and reviewer both say they would repeat, with time and objections recorded.
- The buyer requests a second test in writing within 30 days. A buyer pilot fee is recorded as stronger commercial evidence but is not required for the first mechanism pass.

### Stopping rules

- Stop after discovery if repeated costly uncertainty does not appear.
- Stop if the desired outcome is detection, surveillance, or proof of human authorship.
- Mark access Inconclusive rather than Fail only if no valid hiring event appears within eight weeks for reasons unrelated to the protocol.
- Stop or redesign after a valid fail, including excessive candidate or reviewer burden.
- Allow at most one replacement event and one technical retry.
- Do not build product features merely because the interviews produce enthusiasm.
